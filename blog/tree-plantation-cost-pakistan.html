What Does It Really Cost to Plant a Tree in Pakistan?

19 October 2026 By Ahmad Baloch, Founder ShajarDost 7 min read

Ask most plantation campaigns what a tree costs and you will get a sapling price: Rs.30, Rs.50, maybe Rs.100. It is a comfortable number, and it is misleading. A sapling without three summers of watering is a firewood stick in waiting. We run our own plantation and care operations around Pattoki in Kasur district, and we keep an actual ledger. This article shares our measured numbers for 500 trees under care — not to scare donors, but because honest arithmetic is the only thing standing between "one million trees planted" and "one million trees alive".

The ledger: 500 trees, one full year

Here is what it actually took to keep 500 young trees alive on our care sites for one year — the same figures we use internally to price our donation packages. Nothing is hidden, and nothing is rounded to look nice:

Cost headWhat it coversAnnual cost (Rs.)
Watchman / guardProtection from grazing, theft, broken branches — the single biggest killer of young trees240,000
WaterTanker trips and irrigation for the dry months (October–June is effectively no-rain season)96,000
ElectricityPumping and site lighting share30,000
Fertilizer & soil careKhaad and amendments at the start of each season15,000

ReplacementsThe honest line item most campaigns delete — saplings that did not make it, replanted10,000
Total500 trees, one year401,000

Divide by 500 and you get Rs.802 per tree per year in care alone. Add the one-time planting cost (pit digging, soil prep, the sapling itself — about Rs.150), and the first year of one tree is roughly Rs.950. Three years of establishment care — the period after which a native tree can usually stand on its own — comes to about Rs.2,556 per tree.

Why the watchman costs more than the water

The table surprises people: protection is 60% of the total, three times the water bill. But walk any Punjab roadside in June and look at the young plantation rows — the ones that died were rarely thirsty. They were chewed by goats, cut for firewood, snapped by carts, or trunk-crushed by the very footpath they were supposed to shade. In our experience grazing and human damage kill more young trees than drought does. Money spent on a guard is not overhead; it is the difference between a forest and a repeated purchase of saplings.

Water is the second big truth. Punjab's planting season is generous, but its rainfall is not: beyond the July–August monsoon, nine months of effective drought mean tanker trips or pump electricity for every unprotected site. This is why species choice (see our Punjab species guide) and mulching matter so much — a neem or kikar that survives on residual moisture is far cheaper to keep alive than a water-loving exotic.

What this means for donors

When a campaign says "Rs.100 plants a tree", it is telling you the price of the sapling, not the price of the tree. Our donation packages on the donate page are built around the measured numbers above: roughly Rs.1,200 covers one tree's full first year (planting plus establishment care), Rs.2,200 covers two years, and Rs.3,200 covers the full three-year establishment period, after which the tree's survival is essentially self-sustaining. Larger bundles and our Sadaqah package are priced off the same ledger — our Sadaqah bundle deliberately sits at cost.

Planting a tree is a three-year promise, not a one-day photo. Jo shaadi ki tasveer utaar kar bhool jate hain, wo darakht nahi lagate — sirf baghair any wali lagate hain.

Two honest footnotes for transparency. First, scale improves everything: these figures come from 500 trees across sites where fixed costs (a guard's salary, a pump) are shared. As dense plantings grow to thousands of trees on single sites, per-tree care falls sharply — this is one more reason we favour the dense Miyawaki-style clusters our programme uses. Second, partner sites reduce cost further: where a school or institution provides the land and shares watering duty, the care bill shrinks without shrinking the care.

How to spend less and still keep trees alive

  • Plant in the monsoon window (July–August) or cool season, never before a Punjab summer.
  • Choose drought-hardy natives — neem, kikar, sohanjna, amaltas — for unwatered sites.
  • Mulch heavily at planting; it cuts watering frequency dramatically through April–June.
  • Protect first, water second. A Rs.2,000-per-month guard share on a 200-tree site is cheaper than replanting 60% of it next spring.
  • Plant in clusters, not scattered rows — one watering circuit serves many trees, and neighbours watch each other's saplings.

If you take one number from this article, take this: a tree is not Rs.100; a tree is about Rs.950 in its first year and about Rs.2,556 until it can stand alone. Everything we price, promise and publish starts from that ledger.
