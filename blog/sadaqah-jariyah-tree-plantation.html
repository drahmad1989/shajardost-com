Sadaqah Jariyah: Why Planting a Tree Never Stops Giving

19 October 2026 By Ahmad Baloch, Founder ShajarDost 6 min read

Most charity is spent once. You feed a person — the meal is eaten. You hand over clothes — they wear out. Islam describes a different category of giving called sadaqah jariyah: ongoing charity whose reward keeps flowing long after your hands have let go. And of all the examples the Prophet ﷺ gave for this kind of charity, one of the most beautiful is a tree.

The hadith, in its own words

"There is none amongst the Muslims who plants a tree or sows seeds, and then a bird, or a person or an animal eats from it, but is regarded as a charitable gift (sadaqah) for him."

— Narrated by Anas ibn Malik ﷺ · Sahih al-Bukhari 2320 & Sahih Muslim 1553

Look carefully at what this narration includes: a bird, a person, an animal. The reward is not tied to how big the tree grows or how much fruit it yields for you. Any creature that benefits — the crow that rests on the branch, the goat that eats a fallen leaf, the child who takes shade under the canopy — writes a separate entry of reward for the person who planted it. A tree planted once can be feeding strangers, birds and cattle for decades, and every single day of benefit keeps crediting the planter.

In another narration, the Prophet ﷺ said that when a person dies, their deeds come to an end except three: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, and a righteous child who prays for them (Sahih Muslim). A tree fits the first category perfectly — it is the rare form of charity that literally keeps living and growing after you are gone. Imagine the account that keeps receiving reward through decades of shade, fruit and habitat, even while the planter sleeps in their grave.

"Even if the Hour arrives" — the urgency of planting

"If the Final Hour comes while you have a shoot of a palm-tree in your hands — plant it."

— Musnad Ahmad

This narration is remarkable for its sense of priority. Even at the very end of time — when nothing else could logically matter anymore — the instruction is to plant the sapling you are holding. Scholars explain the lesson: an act of goodness is never wasted, and hope is never to be abandoned. For us the practical takeaway is simpler and closer to home: do not postpone planting. The best season, the perfect plot, the ideal savings — these excuses can wait forever, and saplings do not.

What a tree gives, year after year

It is worth being concrete about what one surviving tree does in a Punjabi city or village over its life, because this is exactly the "ongoing" part of sadaqah jariyah:

  • Shade — for the student sitting under it, the shopkeeper beside it, the animals tethered near it. In 45°C June heat, shade is not a luxury.
  • Food — fruit trees like jamun, mulberry and moringa feed children, birds and livestock for generations.
  • Cleaner air — leaves catch dust and smog particles; in cities like Lahore, which IQAir has repeatedly ranked among the world's most polluted, every leaf is a tiny air filter.
  • Habitat — birds nest, insects pollinate, and the soil beneath fills with life. This is the "bird eats from it" of the hadith, playing out in real time.
  • Water balance and cooling — canopy cover lowers ground temperature and reduces the heat-island effect in dense neighbourhoods.

No one-time gift on this list runs out. The meal is eaten and forgotten; the tree stands and gives for fifty years.

A honest note from our team

The hadith promise is for the tree that survives. A sapling that dries up a month after a photo-op planting gives shade to no one. That is why our donation packages include years of measured care — watering, protection, replacement — and why we tell every donor the real cost of care upfront (roughly Rs.800 per tree per year on our sites). Sadaqah jariyah deserves to be done properly the first time. See the full breakdown in our article on what tree care really costs.

How to start your sadaqah jariyah today

Three practical paths, depending on your situation. First, plant where you already have land — a home courtyard, a school boundary, a village pathway. Choose a hardy native species (our Punjab species guide lists ten proven ones) and commit to watering it for the first two summers. Second, sponsor trees for land you don't own: our team plants and cares for donated trees on partner sites — schools, mosques, hospitals — and shares photos as they establish; our Sadaqah package was priced specifically to cover planting at cost, without a margin.

Third, if money is tight, remember that the hadith of Bukhari and Muslim does not mention money at all — it mentions planting. Request free plants through our Free Plants Wall, plant them yourself, and the ongoing reward described in the narration is yours. The door of sadaqah jariyah is deliberately open at every budget: the rich sponsor forests, the student plants one sapling with his own hands — and the bird, the animal and the passing stranger complete the rest.
