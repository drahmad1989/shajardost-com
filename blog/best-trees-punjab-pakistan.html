10 Best Trees to Plant in Punjab, Pakistan

19 October 2026 By Ahmad Baloch, Founder ShajarDost 9 min read

Wrong tree, wrong place — it is the most common and most expensive mistake we see in plantation drives across Punjab. A species that cannot survive June's 45°C without endless watering, or roots that crack a boundary wall three years later, turns a good intention into a dead sapling or a costly problem. This guide lists ten species we actually plant and care for in the Kasur district — drawn from the Punjab Forest Department's own plantation lists and our own field experience — with honest notes on where each one fits.

The ten species, in detail

NativeThorny

1. Kikar / Babul — Acacia nilotica

The workhorse of Punjab's arid ground. Kikar shrugs off alkaline soils, drought and neglect that kill pampered species, fixes nitrogen in the soil, and gives dense fuelwood and gum. The Punjab Forest Department lists it among its core plantation species for the province. Plant it on village waste land, canal banks and boundary rows — but keep it away from playgrounds and walkways, because its thorns are serious.

NativeTimber Value

2. Shisham / Tahli — Dalbergia sissoo

Punjab's prized timber tree and the backbone of the famous Changa Manga plantation. Straight trunk, deep roots, beautiful dappled shade — the classic avenue and farmland tree. One honest caution: stressed shisham stands across the province have suffered dieback disease in recent decades, so avoid planting it as a single-species row; mix it with mulberry, kikar or sukh chain the way we do in our dense plantings, and it stays healthy and valuable.

OrnamentalBee-Friendly

3. Amaltas — Cassia fistula

The most rewarding flowering tree in Punjab — in early summer it drops its leaves and covers itself entirely in hanging chains of yellow blossoms. Almost every roadside in Lahore sports it. It is clean (no big leaf litter), medium-sized, and perfect for schools and street lines where you want beauty without mess. Its only fault: the first year is slow, and it rewards patience rather than impatience.

MedicinalHardy

4. Neem — Azadirachta indica

If you can only plant one tree for pure public benefit, plant a neem. Year-round dense shade, famous antibacterial and pest-repelling properties, tolerance for polluted air and poor soil, and near-zero water demands once established. This is our default tree for hospital grounds, mosque courtyards and roadside pits. A mature neem cools its surroundings measurably and filters dust — exactly what Punjab's cities need.

Very FastFood

5. Sohanjna — Moringa oleifera

The nutrition tree. Its pods and leaves are a superfood (drumstick curry, moringa powder), it grows shockingly fast from cuttings, tolerates drought, and starts producing within a year. For village households and school nutrition gardens it is unbeatable. Two field notes: plant it where falling branches won't hit anyone (the wood is brittle — prune it young to build a strong frame), and treat it as a productive tree, not a shade giant.

FastBirds Love It

6. Mulberry / Shahtoot — Morus alba

On the Punjab Forest Department's own education list for the province. Mulberry grows fast, holds soil well, feeds silkworms and — more importantly for us — feeds every bird in the neighbourhood when its fruit ripens. A school yard with one mulberry becomes a living bird-watch classroom. Just don't plant it over paved parking: the fallen fruit stains, and pigeons who eat it leave their opinion everywhere.

ShadeNative

7. Sukh Chain — Millettia pinnata

Its name means peace, and that is what it gives: a broad, calm, glossy-canopied shade tree that tolerates heat, poor soil and coastal-level abuse. Lahore's afforestation programme lists it among its standard shade trees. Perfect for boulevards, park edges and large compounds. The pods and leaf litter need a sweep occasionally, which is the only maintenance it will ever ask for.

FloweringSacred

8. Chanar — Crateva adansonii

A beloved old-Punjab tree with cream flower clusters that turn orange, thornless and manageable in size. It shows up on Lahore's official shade-tree list for good reason — it gives compound shade without bullying the buildings around it. Ideal for home compounds, temple-gurdwara-mosque surroundings and neighbourhood parks. Slowish to start; give it two summers of care and it settles in for decades.

Fast CanopyBig Tree

9. Semal / Sumbhal — Bombax ceiba

When empty ground needs a forest look fast, semal delivers: rocket growth, a huge eventual canopy, and dramatic scarlet flowers in spring that attract clouds of birds. It is a big-tree choice — the spiky young trunk smooths with age, but this tree wants room. Use it on large plots, riverbanks and institutional grounds; never under power lines or beside boundary walls.

FruitLong-Lived

10. Jamun — Syzygium cumini

The memory tree of every Punjabi childhood — deep shade, purple summer fruit, and a lifespan measured in generations. A jamun planted today will shade your children's children. It needs a little more water in establishment and true room to spread (plan for a 25-foot-plus canopy), which makes it perfect for campuses, farms and park corners, not tight courtyards.

Honest cautions — jo aksar nahi batate

  • Safeda (Eucalyptus) is not "free shade". It grows fast and the market loves its wood, but it drinks heavily and can draw down water tables and suppress undergrowth near farmland. Foresters increasingly discourage it near crops and wells. If you plant it, keep it away from irrigated fields and water sources.
  • Season matters more than species. The best planting windows in Punjab are the monsoon (July–August) and the cool season through February. Summer-planted saplings without a care plan mostly die.
  • Buy from a real nursery. Weak, root-bound poly-bag plants "save" Rs.20 and lose the year. Our mini nursery programme exists precisely because good saplings are half the battle.
  • Care is the real price. Whatever you plant, the first 2–3 summers decide survival — see our measured numbers in the cost article.

Which tree should you plant?

A simple rule from our field experience: match the tree to the water you can honestly give and the space you honestly have. Tight home courtyard → amaltas or chanar. School yard → mulberry, amaltas, jamun. Village waste land → kikar, sohanjna, semal. Roadside or hospital → neem, sukh chain. Farmland boundary → shisham mixed with mulberry and kikar — never one species alone. And if your community wants trees but has no budget, put a request on our Free Plants Wall — that is exactly what it exists for.
